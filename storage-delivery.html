Customer Care & Handling Guide

PROPER STORAGE. BETTER FOOD HANDLING.

Simple information to help you store frozen products correctly and understand our delivery process.

Why Proper Frozen Storage Matters

PRESERVING TEXTURE, TASTE & QUALITY AT HOME.

Frozen snacks, parathas, vegetables and ready-to-cook non-vegetarian items rely on consistent freezer storage to maintain their intended texture, moisture balance and culinary quality until the moment you cook them.

Placing items promptly into your freezer after purchase and keeping packs properly sealed prevents moisture loss, ice crystal buildup and unnecessary exposure to warm kitchen air.

Packaging First Principle: Every product carries specific storage, defrosting and cooking instructions printed by the manufacturer on its retail pack. Always refer to the pack label first.
Essential Guidelines

STORE IT RIGHT.

Follow these six practical habits to keep your frozen snacks, parathas, vegetables and proteins in optimal ready-to-cook condition.

Follow Printed Instructions

Always follow the storage instructions printed on the product packaging, as different categories (such as parathas, vegetables or seafood) may have specific care notes.

Use a Suitable Freezer

Keep frozen products in a dedicated freezer compartment rather than a standard chiller tray so products remain thoroughly frozen until preparation.

Manufacturer Temperature Guidance

Maintain the freezer temperature recommended by the manufacturer on the individual product label to preserve food quality over time.

Keep Products Properly Sealed

After opening a multi-piece pack of snacks, parathas or vegetables, reseal the pouch tightly or transfer unused portions into an airtight freezer-safe container.

Avoid Temperature Fluctuations

Take out only the portion you plan to cook and return the remaining pack to the freezer immediately to avoid unnecessary temperature fluctuations.

Do Not Refreeze Thawed Items

Do not refreeze thawed products unless the product instructions specifically permit it. Once thawed, prepare and consume according to label advice.

Safe Kitchen Practices

RESPONSIBLE PRODUCT HANDLING BEFORE & DURING COOKING.

Good kitchen habits help you get the best results from every pack of frozen food while keeping meal preparation safe and hygienic.

1

Check Packaging Before Storage

Inspect pouches and boxes before placing them in the freezer to ensure seals are intact and undamaged.

2

Read Expiry & Best-Before Details

Check manufacturing, expiry and best-before dates printed on the pack and organize your freezer so older packs are used first.

3

Follow Cooking Instructions

Some products are designed to be cooked directly from frozen (such as samosas, nuggets and parathas), while others may require thawing—follow the exact method on the pack.

4

Avoid Damaged or Improperly Stored Products

Avoid consuming products if the primary packaging is torn, unsealed or shows signs of improper storage prior to cooking.

5

Handle According to Packaging Guidance

Keep raw non-vegetarian ready-to-cook products separate from ready-to-eat foods and wash hands and utensils thoroughly after handling.

Helpful Answers

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS.

Find answers to common questions about storing frozen foods, checking pack instructions and submitting delivery enquiries.

Place frozen products directly into a suitable freezer compartment as soon as possible after purchase. Always follow the specific storage instructions and temperature guidance printed by the manufacturer on each product pack, and keep opened packs tightly resealed.

Frozen foods should not be left at room temperature for extended periods except when thawing specifically as directed by the product packaging prior to cooking. Leaving frozen items outside the freezer causes thawing and affects food quality and safety.

Detailed cooking methods—such as pan-heating, shallow frying, deep frying, air-frying, steaming or baking—along with recommended cooking times are printed on the back or side panel of each individual product package.

Do not refreeze thawed products unless the manufacturer's instructions on the product packaging explicitly state that it is safe to do so. To avoid waste, remove only the number of pieces or portion size you need for a single meal and return the rest to the freezer immediately.
